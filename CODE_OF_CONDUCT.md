# Code of Conduct

JX Manifesto is a public professional conversation about the conditions in which journalism is produced. Participants may disagree strongly, but must make that disagreement useful and safe.

## Expected behavior

- Critique ideas, wording, evidence, tools, and workflows rather than people.
- Make room for journalists, translators, researchers, designers, and developers with different levels of technical experience.
- State relevant context, uncertainty, conflicts of interest, and limits of evidence.
- Protect confidential sources, newsroom information, and personal data.
- Respect language, regional, cultural, and professional differences.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, discriminatory language, or personal attacks.
- Publishing private information without permission.
- Pressuring contributors to identify a newsroom, source, or colleague.
- Misrepresenting anecdotal experience as universal or validated evidence.
- Spam, promotion unrelated to JX, or repeated disruption of review.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, pause a discussion, reject a contribution, or restrict participation when behavior violates this policy. Decisions should be proportionate and documented when doing so would not expose a reporter or affected person.

For sensitive reports that should not be public, email **javid.momeni@gmail.com** with the subject “JX Code of Conduct”. Reports will be handled as privately as practical. Anyone directly involved in a report should not make the final enforcement decision alone.

This policy applies to the repository, issue forms, pull requests, discussions, and project-related public communication.
