# JX research notes

These notes separate empirical and professional sources from the normative manifesto. A source being listed here does not mean it proves a JX principle. It identifies evidence, concepts, limitations, or questions that informed the working framework.

JX is not yet a validated measurement instrument, certification, causal model, or scientific score. Claims about a specific newsroom intervention should be tested in context and should not be inferred from adjacent research alone.

## Attention, interruption, and cognitive load

- Gloria Mark, Daniela Gudith, and Ulrich Klocke, [“The Cost of Interrupted Work: More Speed and Stress”](https://doi.org/10.1145/1357054.1357072), CHI 2008. An experimental study of interruption and compensatory work speed, stress, frustration, effort, and time pressure. It supports treating interruption as a cost, but it was not conducted in a newsroom and does not prescribe a specific interface pattern.

**JX use:** informs questions about context switching, recoverability, notification design, hidden memory work, and whether speed gains transfer cost to people.

## Occupational stress, burnout, and working conditions

- Susana Monteiro, Alexandra Marques Pinto, and Magda Sofia Roberto, [“Job demands, coping, and impacts of occupational stress among journalists: a systematic review”](https://doi.org/10.1080/1359432X.2015.1114470), 2016. Reviews empirical work on journalists’ occupational stressors, coping, health, and wellbeing.
- Scott Reinardy, [“Newspaper journalism in crisis: Burnout on the rise, eroding young journalists’ career commitment”](https://doi.org/10.1177/1464884910385188), 2011. A survey study of newspaper journalists that connects burnout dimensions with intention to leave. Its population and media period limit generalization to every contemporary newsroom.
- Jasmine B. MacDonald et al., [“Journalists and Depressive Symptoms: A Systematic Literature Review”](https://doi.org/10.1177/15248380211016022), 2021/2023. Reviews quantitative studies and identifies trauma, threat, support, and methodological gaps.

**JX use:** supports treating working conditions, support, workload, safety, and professional wellbeing as system concerns. These sources do not establish that a UI change prevents burnout or mental illness.

## Digital newsroom systems, metrics, and collaboration

- Kathryn Hayes and Michelle O’Sullivan, [“Labouring the news: Management control strategies and work intensification in the digital newsroom”](https://doi.org/10.1177/14648849231174059), 2024. A qualitative case study of management control, analytics, workload, and newsroom practice.
- Mel Bunce, Kate Wright, and Martin Scott, [“‘Our newsroom in the cloud’: Slack, virtual newsrooms and journalistic practice”](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444817748955), 2018. An ethnographic study showing both collaborative benefits and boundary or culture risks in a virtual newsroom.
- Colin Porlezza, [“The datafication of digital journalism”](https://doi.org/10.1177/14648849231190232), 2024. Reviews datafication and ethical or regulatory questions involving accountability, transparency, and privacy.

**JX use:** informs the decision to assess tools together with policies, metrics, incentives, roles, handoffs, and information flows rather than treating interface usability as the whole system.

## Human–AI collaboration and editorial accountability

- Bronwyn Jones, Rhianne Jones, and Ewa Luger, [“AI ‘Everywhere and Nowhere’: Addressing the AI Intelligibility Problem in Public Service Journalism”](https://doi.org/10.1080/21670811.2022.2145328), 2023. Examines how opaque AI systems can disrupt accountability, professional judgment, and journalists’ ability to understand or challenge system behavior.
- Matt Carlson, [“Automating judgment? Algorithmic judgment, news knowledge, and journalistic professionalism”](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444817706684), 2018. Distinguishes algorithmic judgment from professional journalistic judgment and questions assumptions of algorithmic objectivity.
- Hannes Cools and Nicholas Diakopoulos, [“Uses of Generative AI in the Newsroom: Mapping Journalists’ Perceptions of Perils and Possibilities”](https://doi.org/10.1080/17512786.2024.2394558), 2024. Interview research on actual and anticipated uses, efficiency, credibility, bias, literacy, and monitoring.
- UNESCO, [Regional Declaration on the Ethical and Transparent Use of Artificial Intelligence in the Media](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-and-media-ethics-press-councils-south-east-europe-and-turkiye-adopt-landmark-declaration), 2025. A professional and self-regulatory reference emphasizing transparency, editorial accountability, risk assessment, and human judgment; it is guidance, not empirical validation.

**JX use:** informs requirements for visible provenance, uncertainty, human approval, reviewability, and named editorial responsibility in AI-assisted work.

## Evidence gaps and research agenda

JX needs direct, comparative newsroom research across languages, regions, organization sizes, employment conditions, and risk contexts. Priority questions include:

1. Which workflow frictions measurably affect verification quality, correction rates, lost context, time, or perceived control?
2. Which interventions reduce interruption and rework without hiding important signals or increasing another role’s burden?
3. How should human–AI provenance and approval be represented so that journalists can challenge outputs under real deadline pressure?
4. Which indicators are useful for team learning without becoming surveillance or individual productivity scores?
5. How do Persian-language, low-bandwidth, small-team, freelance, and high-risk newsrooms differ from the populations represented in current research?

## خلاصه فارسی

این سند منابع تجربی و حرفه‌ای را از متن هنجاری منشور جدا می‌کند. حضور یک منبع به معنی اثبات یک اصل JX نیست. JX هنوز ابزار سنجش اعتبارسنجی‌شده، گواهی، مدل علّی یا امتیاز علمی نیست و اثر هر مداخله باید در بافت واقعی تحریریه آزموده شود.

چهار حوزه فعلی پژوهش عبارت‌اند از توجه و وقفه، فشار شغلی و فرسودگی، سامانه و سنجه‌های تحریریه دیجیتال، و همکاری انسان و هوش مصنوعی. شکاف مهم، کمبود پژوهش مقایسه‌ای مستقیم در زبان‌ها، مناطق، اندازه‌های سازمان، شرایط استخدامی و تحریریه‌های کم‌منبع یا پرریسک است.
